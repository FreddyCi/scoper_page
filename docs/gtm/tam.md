## How the two axes compound

Every prior thread in this conversation reduces to two independent filters multiplying against each other:

**Trade axis** (which subs) — Division 07 envelope trades (roofing, waterproofing, siding, sheet metal) first, since the Scoper generalizes almost for free within a spec division → Division 09 finishes (drywall, painting, flooring) second → Division 03/04 (concrete, masonry) third → Industrial-grade trades (electrical, mechanical, fire protection) much later, if ever, since that buyer is already tech-enabled and better served.

**Sector axis** (which projects) — Commercial first (matches your DPR–Summit Ridge MSA example, lowest compliance load, fastest sales cycle) → Institutional & Public second (hardest documents, but where AI extraction earns the most per bid — bonding, prevailing wage, MBE/WBE clauses) → Industrial non-megaproject opportunistically → Civil/Infrastructure (CM/GC) and Industrial megaprojects (data centers) off the roadmap entirely, since both involve fundamentally different workflows and buyer sophistication.

**Stage 1** = Roofing × Commercial only ($6M–$10M). **Stage 2** = expand trade axis while holding sector fixed (envelope + finishes trades, still Commercial, $60M-$100M+ TAM slice). **Stage 2b** = expand sector axis while holding proven trades fixed (same trades, now also Institutional/Public). Full SAM capture would mean both axes fully expanded simultaneously — which is the $150M-$250M SAM figure, and realistically a 5+ year outcome, not a 3-year one.
